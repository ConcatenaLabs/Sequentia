# The incident is closed, the bug it exposed is fixed, and 24.3.0 is good to go

Follow-up to today's letter on the empty blocks, summarising everything found and done since. The short version: your report was accurate, the cause is fully understood, the one real defect it exposed is fixed in 24.3.0 itself, and the flag day now stands at **block 101,810** with about a day of runway from the tag. Nothing observed today argues for holding it, and the strongest evidence is that your own node's symptom is reproduced, explained, and cured by the release you would be adopting.

## What actually happened on the network

The pause you placed on asset `d1bd783c…` at block 99879 (10:13:44Z) invalidated your node's entire subsequent transaction flow: every spend of the paused asset, and every transaction whose fee inputs were change of one. Our nodes rejected each of them correctly and silently, because rejection logging (`mempoolrej`) is off by default; it is now enabled at the public entry. Your node's last transaction that validated here arrived at 10:13:04Z, forty seconds before the pause confirmed, and the timestamp is identical on every link between our host and yours.

The producers were healthy the entire time. All 20 run one uniform binary (master commit `5e6a302d8`, version 24.1.0), their mempools were empty throughout, no eviction ever fired, and every block arrived on schedule, which your own letter observed. Blocks 99880 through 100261 were empty because nothing valid was offered: your flow was invalidated and our host's services happened to be idle. The moment anything valid appeared it confirmed immediately. Inclusion was then verified end to end through four independent entry points, each confirming in one to three blocks: local RPC (height 100262), public HTTP (100274), a second RPC probe under full debug logging (100277), and pure P2P from a fresh external node synced from genesis over the public port, your exact topology (100306).

Your stuck unpause fits the same picture, and the unpause path itself is consensus-clean: the record script is exempt from the frozen-spend rule (it is never a single-owner script), so a pause can always be lifted. The transaction almost certainly stalled because its fee inputs were drawn from unconfirmed change of the stuck family. Rebuild it funding the fee from confirmed, untangled coins and it will confirm; or rebroadcast the existing hex through the explorer and read the exact rejection reason from the log.

## The real bug your report led to, and its fix

Your mempool retaining day-old invalidated spends was the loose end, and it turned out to be a genuine defect on our side rather than anything strange on yours. `mempool.dat` is loaded through acceptance checks whose supervision filter is only as strong as the registry at that moment, and the registry rebuild at startup races the thread that loads the file. A file written by a binary that predates supervision eviction carries dead spends outright. Once such an entry was admitted there was no later cure: the steady-state eviction runs only when a connecting block carries a supervision record, so a node that starts poisoned stays poisoned indefinitely, re-announces the dead spend on request, and chains wallet children onto its change.

That sequence reconciles everything in your letter. A node that ran an older binary through the pause kept the spends and wrote them to disk; upgrading to a binary containing `20099a8` then re-imported the residue through this exact race. Your node can truthfully contain the eviction commit and truthfully hold invalidated spends at the same time.

The fix is merged as **PR #143**: one supervision sweep of the mempool immediately after the load completes, closing the invariant regardless of which binary wrote the file or which thread won the race. It is proven in both directions. The regression test plants a frozen spend and an honest control directly into a hand-crafted `mempool.dat`; against the previous binary it fails exactly as your incident predicts (the load reports both entries admitted, the frozen spend sits resident), and against the fixed binary the honest entry loads and mines while the frozen one never surfaces. **PR #144** additionally verified the reorg paths empirically, each with a control transaction so an absence can never pass vacuously: the record block disconnected lifts the pause, reconnected it evicts again, and a losing-branch spend of a paused asset is never admitted by the reorg while an ordinary spend from the same block is.

## Why 24.3.0 is safe for you, and what it does for your node

- **The flag day is 101,810**, re-derived at tag time as tip plus 1,440 blocks rounded up, so the runway is a true day rather than a number that decayed while branches waited. The tag exists and the download pipeline is publishing from it.
- **The budget change shares no mechanism with inclusion.** It reprices Simplicity witness data; today's incident lived entirely in mempool admission and one node's retained state, and the producers never missed a slot even at the worst of it.
- **Your acceptance test gates the cutover, in your hands.** Before the committee is stopped, a fresh probe goes through the public entry and must confirm within three blocks while the old binaries are still producing; a failure holds everything with nothing to unwind. Broadcast your own probe too, funded from confirmed coins with the fee in tSEQ, and judge the network by it. If a hold is ever needed after deployment, the path is written: a one-line height bump, tag v24.3.1, same cutover, a maintainer's decision.
- **Upgrading is itself your cure.** The first start of 24.3.0 sweeps your retained residue out of the mempool. After that, lift the pause through your issuer flow if the test is complete, rebuild anything that funded fees from stuck change, and rebroadcast the remainder; each transaction will either confirm or produce a logged, quotable rejection.

Two things remain useful to send, neither of them blocking: the txid of your original high-fee probe, so its rejection can be matched against the now-enabled logging, and your node's exact build string, to confirm the residue story against a known binary. Go ahead with 24.3.0.
